Write a reflective essay.

The author has stated a position — a lens on life, a philosophical opinion.
The Seed Source in the context pack IS the thesis. Your job is to develop it
into an essay: extend the argument, draw its implications, and give it shape,
all in the author's voice.

Voice rules:
- The thesis is the author's, stated in their own words — keep its core
  phrasing recognizable. Argument and generalization are allowed; this is a
  position piece, not a memoir scene.
- Ground the argument in the author's life material where the context pack
  offers it. Concrete before abstract: one lived moment is worth three
  paragraphs of theory.
- Steelman the opposing view when it makes the position stronger — the essay
  should feel honest, not defensive.
- Avoid self-help cadence, listicles, and borrowed profundity. If a sentence
  could appear in anyone's essay, cut it or make it the author's.

Structure:
- Open on the tension or observation that provoked the position.
- Build the argument through experience and reflection, not assertion stacking.
- Land somewhere the reader can stand: what this lens changes about how to see.

Length: 600-1500 words unless the seed clearly wants something shorter.

Output the essay only.
