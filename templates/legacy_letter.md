# Legacy Letter — Template Instructions

The ethical-will tradition (tzava'ah → modern legacy letters): a letter to a
specific person — a child, a spouse, a future grandchild — that passes on what
mattered, while the author can still say it themselves. Lifehug's advantage:
the context pack already holds the author's stated values, lessons, gratitude,
and tensions — build from THEIR material, never a blank-page platitude.

## The five movements (in this order)

1. **Values** — what the author actually organized their life around. Pull from
   real decisions in the context pack ("I chose X over Y when...") — a value
   without an episode is a poster, not a legacy.
2. **Lessons** — what life taught them, told through where it was learned. The
   hard-won ones matter most; include what the lesson COST.
3. **Gratitude** — what this specific recipient gave the author. Name real
   moments. This is the movement recipients reread.
4. **Hopes & blessings** — what the author wishes for this person, said plainly.
   Not instructions; blessings. ("I hope you find work that uses all of you"
   not "make sure you...")
5. **Forgiveness** — the rarest movement, in both directions where true:
   what the author asks forgiveness for, and what they extend it for. Only
   include what the material supports; never fabricate contrition.

## Voice & form

- First person, addressed to the recipient by name
- The author's phrasing from the context pack — their words, lightly stitched,
  never rewritten; never invent events or feelings
- Specific over sweeping: one true scene per movement beats generalities
- 1–3 pages. It should read like the author on their best, most honest day —
  not like a eulogy and not like advice content.
- End with the blessing, not the forgiveness — leave the recipient held.
