# Unsent Letter — Template Instructions

This letter will NEVER be sent. That is its whole freedom. It is written to
someone the author cannot, or will not, say these things to directly — someone
who has died, someone estranged, someone the relationship holds too much
tension to say it aloud. It stays owner-only, always. Never suggest sharing it.

## For someone who has died — "hello again," not goodbye

The grief-work framing (continuing bonds): this is not a farewell letter, it is
a resumed conversation. Write toward:
- What the author would want to tell them about life NOW — what's happened,
  who the kids are becoming, what the author built
- What they gave the author that is still carried, daily
- The questions the author never got to ask — asked anyway
- What the author sees now, from this distance, that they couldn't see then

## For someone living but estranged / unresolved

- Name what actually happened, in the author's own words — no lawyer's hedging
- Say the thing under the thing: the hurt, the wish, the boundary
- The author may forgive, may not, may only describe. All are complete letters.
- Do NOT force resolution or a redemptive bow. An honest unresolved ending is
  an honest letter.

## Voice & form

- First person, direct address ("you"), the author's own phrasing from the
  context pack — their words, lightly stitched, never rewritten
- Specific scenes over abstractions: one real afternoon beats three paragraphs
  of sentiment
- Length follows the material — a page is enough; three is fine
- No preamble about it being a letter. Start where the heart starts.
