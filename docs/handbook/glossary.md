# Glossary

Every standard term, defined once. Feature pages link here instead of
re-defining. Seeded verbatim from the README's Nomenclature section at
v171; as feature pages land, terms they own move here as the single
definition and the README slims to a pointer.

The wiki is a **graph of your life**, and these are the standard terms used throughout:

- **Node** — a graph vertex: a durable subject in your life that can be compiled into a wiki page. People, places, periods, objects, themes, projects, and *you* are nodes.
- **Node Type** — graph vocabulary for the kind of node, such as person, place, period, object, theme, project, or life. Most current `Entity Type` values are node types; `relationship` is the exception because it represents an edge page.
- **Entity** — the current product/code term for a node-worthy subject; usually one wiki page. Keep using Entity in code and product flows where the system already does.
- **Entity Type** — the current product/code and frontmatter term for a wiki page kind: `person`, `place`, `period`, `object`, `theme`, `project`, `relationship`, and `life` (you). Most entity types are node types; `relationship` remains the compatibility page type for an edge page.
- **Edge** — a meaningful connection between nodes/entities. An edge can carry evidence, tension, change over time, and artifact relevance.
- **Relationship Edge** — a human bond edge, usually between you and another person. The page in `wiki/relationships/` is an edge page: it answers what the bond is, not merely who the other person is.
- **Focus** — an entity you're deliberately building toward a deliverable (book, letter, …), with a tier and target. **You are the primary Focus** — your own life story is the biggest one and gets the largest share of questions; self-knowledge (values, fears, contradictions, growth) is a built-in dimension of it, not a separate track.
- **Project** — a Focus whose deliverable is a composite piece built up over time. Today that's the book: your categories become chapters. A project is virtual while you're planning it — readiness is computed live from the roadmap and answered material — and becomes a concrete, versioned piece once `book-assemble` stitches the latest chapter drafts together.
- **Piece** — a single versioned work in the Studio: a letter, tweet, essay, or chapter draft. Lives under `outputs/<slug>/` as `v1.md`, `v2.md`, ... revisions, with AI-assisted revise, mark-final, and promote-back-to-source. The code/CLI term is unchanged: **artifact** (`system/lifehug.py artifact ...`, `sources/artifacts/`).
- **Studio** — the single workspace for making pieces and projects: grouped by Focus, project cards expand into a chapter table, piece cards keep their version/revision history, and a create form starts new pieces.
- **Entity graduation / node graduation** — the wiki grows itself: entities mentioned across your answers are detected, **AI-curated** into a roster (`lifehug.py entity-roster --type <t>`), and graduated into node pages built from those mentions. Places and periods graduate on a low bar (a few mentions); **objects** graduate on AI-judged symbolic meaning (e.g. *The Cleats*), not frequency; people on score; **themes** via an AI-curated keyword roster (v97) — new themes like *Parenting* emerge from opinions, essays, and classifier extractions. Relationship edges use a dyadic path: Focus relationships can graduate from dedicated answers or enough cross-story mentions about the person. Rosters refresh monthly; compile graduates the current roster entries into pages — no manual work.
- **The Loop** — the canonical continuous-learning cycle: capture source → compile wiki → lint/repair source truth → classify/score signals → promote candidates and plan the queue → ask a better question → create artifacts → feed final artifacts back as source. When we ask whether a feature "works in the Loop," we mean this path.
- **The three loops** — the whole schedule the Loop runs on: **Daily** (`system/daily_question.sh`) delivers one question and takes the answer, free and model-free; **Weekly** (`system/weekly_maintenance.sh`) learns from the week and rebuilds the queue (compile → source-lint → classify-story → quality-update → judgment-update → timeline-retire → wiki harvest → mirror-compile → candidates-auto-promote → planner-queue → arc-plan → report); **Monthly** (`system/monthly_research.sh`) grows (research-expand → recommend-focuses → focus-autopilot → entity-roster per type → compile → perennials → thread offers → resurfacing → report). Per-answer events run continuously and are not a fourth clock.
- **Question queue** — the cache of the most effective next questions: `planner-queue`'s weekly output, expiring with the week. It is an **aggregation with a formula** (`question_planner.build_queue`, `system/question_planner.py:672`), not a hand-written list — pending bank questions weighted by dynamic Focus allocation and group caps (main 0.50 / project 0.35 / focus 0.25), least-covered category first, ×2.5 objective boost, 0.15 chapter-gap fraction, story-function caps (scene 0.45 … output_gap 0.20), rumination cooldown ×0.25, the escalation gate, and `--arc-max` 2. Questions enter the bank only by promotion; Timeline/Mirror gap findings feed arc cards and curation surfaces, not the bank.
- **In the Loop** — code, state, or docs reached by the daily, weekly, monthly, or artifact flows without a human manually stitching it together, and whose output can affect future questions, wiki pages, relationship understanding, or artifacts.
- **Loop-adjacent** — useful manual, dry-run, inspection, setup, or repair surfaces. They support the Loop but do not change future behavior until their output is promoted into a Loop surface.
- **Out of the Loop** — code or data that exists but is not called by scheduled/manual Loop entrypoints and is not read by downstream Loop state. Mission-critical work should not stay here; wire it in or document it as experimental.
- **Interaction** — a role definition for the AI in one situation: purpose, behavior contract, context recipe, scope, and evals, packaged as files any qualified model can execute. The definition lives in the framework (`interactions/<name>/`); each runtime loads it; a model is "seated" in it only after passing its eval harness. Out-of-scope input is politely deflected. Ten today: **conversation** (chats + longer sessions), **question judgment** (which follow-up candidates deserve to exist, and how urgently — ADR 0007), **focus curation** (judging first-encounter Focus/idea duplicate name variants the deterministic layers can't resolve — ADR 0010), and seven *children* of conversation — **question candidate** (placement, ADR 0018), **focus candidate** (onboarding, ADR 0021), **entity candidate** (identity, ADR 0022), **arc walk** (arc walking, ADR 0023), **timeline** (placing a memory in time, ADR 0024), **landmarks** (the universal dating question set, v197), **reading room** (evidence-driven dating sessions, ADR 0025 — removed 2026-09-03 by owner ruling R4a; deleted in Cut 2c).
- **Child interaction** — an Interaction that `extends` Conversation by exact version and adds exactly ONE goal: a stage-keyed `prompt/turn-instructions.md` leaf the host substitutes into, ONE optional additive structured-output field gated on a `TurnShape` flag, its own lints/goldens/evals harness, and its own seat. Seven exist: placement (v188), onboarding (v189), identity (v190), arc walking (v193), timeline placement (v195), landmarks (v197), the Reading Room (v204). The seventh is the one exception to "exactly ONE additive field", and in the strict direction: it mints NO field of its own and REUSES two — `placed` and `landmark` — behind a single `TurnShape` gate. The paradigm is written once in `interactions/README.md`.
- **Play** — one verb: it **approves** the thing and **starts** its conversation. The approving write (promote the candidate, scaffold the focus, graduate the entity) runs in the host's background job; the conversation opens immediately and never waits on it, so the model states the act once as an aside and takes a correction as a *move*. "Play is read-only" is retired vocabulary (platform ADR 0020).
- **Play target** *(the host's endpoint shape is still proposed — platform issue #570)* — what a Play is pointed at: `{kind, ref, goal, question_ids[], context}` for a candidate, focus, entity, question, chapter, book, or the whole queue. One endpoint, one tab renderer; the daily loop becomes a *scheduled* Play. The package's half is real as of v193: `arc_walk.normalize_target` and `arc_walk.ARC_TARGET_KINDS` cover the five kinds that carry a *set* of questions (focus, chapter, book, category, queue) — a single-question Play is an ordinary chat and needs no plan.
- **Placement aside** — the question-candidate child's one goal: the first reply names, in one plain sentence, the focus the answered question was filed under, and accepts a different place as a move (`placement` output field, v188).
- **Onboarding context** — the focus-candidate child's one goal: establishing what a just-started Focus covers and how far it reaches, so the questions seeded for it are worth asking (`focus_setup` output field, v189).
- **Identity context** — the entity-candidate child's one goal: the names someone goes by, how they are related, whether they are living, and whether the roster already holds them under another name (`entity_setup` output field, v190).
- **Arc plan** — the arc-walking child's map: the ordered OPEN questions for a Play target, ranked by the weekly planner's own weights (`question_planner.enriched_pending_questions` + `build_queue`'s weight expression and `arc_max` streak cap) with this week's arc-card intents attached where they exist. Recomputed at every Play and **never persisted**, so answered questions fall out on their own (`arc_walk.build_arc_plan`, ADR 0023). A map, not a script: the model announces it once and then follows the person.
- **Episode** — one session walking a slice of an arc plan: about 4–8 questions, sized by the target's tier (`arc_walk.episode_size_for` — basic 4 / standard 6 / extreme 8), closing warmly with what was covered and the fact that the rest waits. Never a checklist, never a streak, and nothing is ever "unfinished". The next Play is a new episode of a freshly computed plan.
- **Arc walking** — the fourth child interaction's one goal: work a target's open questions casually, in resumable episodes (`answered_question_id` output field, v193).
- **Date record** — a date as an *interval with a basis*: `{best, earliest, latest, granularity, confidence, basis, anchors, provenance}`, stored in EDTF / ISO 8601-2 level 1 (`1984~`, `198X`, `1998-06`, `2001-21` = spring 2001, `1984/1990`, `1984/..`, `../1984`). The package never asks for a year first and never invents one, but it does record what was said and do the arithmetic on it (`chronology.DateRecord`, ADR 0024). An interval is a finding, not a failure.
- **Basis** — *how* the system came to believe a date: `stated` (they said it), `age` (their age against their birthday), `anchor` (a landmark plus a before/after), `order` (sequence only), `public_event`, `connector`, and the three **evidence** bases the Reading Room adds (v204): `document` (a date printed on paper and read out — the strongest warrant there is, weighted above `stated`, because it is not a reconstruction), `photo` (a contextual date, which is a *window* by construction and says so on the record it writes), and `relative` (someone else's memory, relayed, with the witness named in provenance). Every date record carries one, and the page can therefore show its work — "around 1984 — you said you were about five".
- **Anchor** — the **code** term for what a landmark's date becomes once it can bound something: a row in the derived index other dates hang off — their birthday, a residence with a span, an era with a span, a dated moment (`timeline.anchor_index`, `basis: "anchor"`, `chronology.from_anchor`, `timeline_interaction.anchors_for_person`). Collectively they are the life-history calendar; the `timeline` interaction may lean on and repeat NO date that is not one of them. **A landmark is the question and the answer; an anchor is what the answer turns into** — two things, not two names for one (owner-set, 2026-08-23). *Landmarks* is the user-facing word; *anchor* stays in code. The join is `landmarks_interaction.anchors_from_landmarks`.
- **Unknown** — ONE concrete thing on the timeline the person can answer about: `{kind, key, label, probe}`, where the label names the subject and the probe is a real question about it. Seven kinds: a specific undated **moment**, an era's missing **bounds**, a **place**'s span, an **era gap** (a dated hole between two dated bands — the kind that only exists once dates do), a **date contradiction**, and — v202 — a **landmark subject** (one half-filled person or place inside an enumeration domain, named: "What year was Jackie born?") and a **residence gap** (a hole between two dated residence spans). The last two arrive carrying the ladder's own subject-named question. Never a count: "116 moments I can't place" is a number, and a number is not a question (owner-set, 2026-08-23; the counts live on the **unknown ledger**, `timeline.unknown_ledger`).
- **Keystone** — (see [Timeline](#timeline)) the anchor that would resolve the most unknowns. Leverage is the size of that set, and it is the number the person is shown. The starred set is a **greedy plan over the residual graph**, not a top-two leverage list: take the anchor with the largest gain against what is *still* unknown, remove what it covers, repeat (`go-deep.md` §8.3). Ranking independently by leverage double-counted — on real vault data one star's resolve set was a strict subset of the other's, so the second star's marginal gain was zero. A plan is never padded to the cap; when nothing left adds anything, the shorter plan is the honest one. "One answer would place fourteen moments." Every keystone carries the identity it is asked under, `tl:<anchor-slug>` — a question is a keystone because it IS that probe, never because it sits near one.
- **Landmarks** — the product word, owner-set 2026-08-23, and the same name from the surface down to the file on disk. The universal set of dating questions everyone gets (birth, **family**, residences, schooling, partnerships, children, jobs, military, losses); the skeleton that makes everything else placeable by arithmetic. Their dated results become **anchors** (the code term — see above). Shipped v199 as the sixth child interaction; `family` added in v202 — see [Timeline](#timeline) and `docs/handbook/interactions/landmarks.md`.
- **Family** — the ninth landmark domain (v202, owner-set 2026-08-24): the constellation you came from — siblings, parents, grandparents — asked second, where practitioner intake puts it (research §2.9). One entry per PERSON, ladder `who → relation → birth → living`. **Siblings' birth years are anchors**: a birth year is overlearned semantic knowledge, so it may be asked outright exactly as the person's own birthday may (`YEAR_OPENER_DOMAINS`), and it bounds *childhood*, the stretch age arithmetic can least reach. **Elders are witnesses**: a living parent or grandparent can supply the residence and school chains complete (`witness_candidates`). The *dates* are landmarks; the *people* go to the entity roster as PERSON entries carrying the relationship fact, through the existing `entity-verdict --relationship/--living` verbs — never a parallel family store.
- **Whisper** — (see [Timeline](#timeline)) information woven into a conversation that fits naturally, drawn from an arc that has developed, and serving a **second agenda beyond the conversation's primary one**. A whisper is never a change of subject and never an interrogation: it is raised only where it fits, at most once per conversation, and it is not a cost the loop trades off — a whisper that lands is a gift, not a debt. Owner-set, 2026-08-23: *"a whisper is information woven into a conversation that fits naturally from an arc that's developed and solves some other agenda."* Whispers are a general mechanism; each kind names its own agenda.
- **Timeline whisper** — the first kind of whisper: the same natural-conversation move applied to **placing moments in time**. The week's arc card carries a timeline keystone's real probe, its identity (`tl:<anchor-slug>`), and the person's own landmarks; the conversation raises it where it fits, accepts any precision (a range places things), never presses, and never opens with a calendar year. The answer files through `timeline-place` and the next compile re-derives the timeline.
- **Keystone question** — the same probe minted as an ordinary bank question in the `timeline` group and asked as the day's question. It clears one dial (`timeline_leverage_per_story`, default 6) before it is minted at all; answered once, never re-asked.
- **"I'll find out"** — an ordinary answer. Nothing files, nothing is remembered, the unknown stays outstanding with its star and its Play. (v195's deferral side-state was deleted in v196.)
- **Band** — a row of the timeline: the person's own life **chapter** wherever a dated chapter covers the stretch, the system's **period** everywhere else; places lived nest inside, events sit under their place (`timeline_data()["bands"]`).
- **Current** — the version of a Piece you would hand someone today; the star the CLI still calls `final` (`artifact save --final`). An artifact is never *done*, so the UI word is "current" (platform issue #566 ruling).
- **Foundation** — the approved question bank, browsable by focus and category: the questions that exist. **Review** is its counterpart — what the system grew on its own and is waiting for your eye. **Queue** is the cache of the most effective next questions.
- **Chat** — the short exchange around the daily question: system-initiated, ~3 exchanges, arc-carded, graceful third-turn exit, closing takeaway.
- **Conversation** — a long user-initiated session (a story, "something on your mind", or a thread the system offered); runs the full interviewer arc; closes with a narrative takeaway.
- **Arc card** — the pre-planned skeleton for a chat/conversation: opening framing + 2–4 follow-up *intents* (not scripted text), planned by the loops, executed live per turn.
- **Session** — one bounded run: open → turns → close; the durable record is the session document.

## Timeline

The timeline's vocabulary is one named set — three terms that only make sense
together (owner-set, 2026-08-23).

- **Landmarks** — the **universal** set of dating questions everyone gets: birth, the places lived, schooling, partnerships, children, jobs. They are the same for every person, and they are the skeleton that makes everything else placeable by arithmetic — knowing a birthday and a stated age *is* a year; knowing when you lived somewhere dates every moment that happened there. Some are answered at onboarding, in **generalities**; the rest sit under the Timeline as always-open rows with Play; a landmark question may enter the daily queue or a whisper when it passes the shared value threshold (owner ruling R2, 2026-09-03, `lifehug-platform docs/decisions/2026-09-03-timeline-unification/decision-record.md`; lands in Cut 5b, tracking #573/#586); it is never a reminder and never nags. Each domain has a **specificity ladder** (residence: city → address → span → household), so a vague answer is an *answer* and the row stays open only because more would unlock more. In code the *dated results* of these questions are the **anchors** every probe hangs on (`timeline.anchor_index`, `chronology.from_age` / `from_anchor`), and filling that index is the whole point — before v197 `birth_date` was a parameter no caller passed. The set is data: `interactions/landmarks/questions.yaml`. Research: `system/research/landmarks.md`. Contract: `docs/pr-specs/landmarks.md`.
- **Family** — the ninth landmark domain (v202, owner-set 2026-08-24): the constellation you came from — siblings, parents, grandparents — asked second, where practitioner intake puts it (research §2.9). One entry per PERSON, ladder `who → relation → birth → living`. **Siblings' birth years are anchors**: a birth year is overlearned semantic knowledge, so it may be asked outright exactly as the person's own birthday may (`YEAR_OPENER_DOMAINS`), and it bounds *childhood*, the stretch age arithmetic can least reach. **Elders are witnesses**: a living parent or grandparent can supply the residence and school chains complete (`witness_candidates`). The *dates* are landmarks; the *people* go to the entity roster as PERSON entries carrying the relationship fact, through the existing `entity-verdict --relationship/--living` verbs — never a parallel family store.
- **Cross-dating** — the mechanic the landmark set enables (`system/research/go-deep.md` §7): dating an undated sequence by matching it against an already-dated one. The landmarks are the dated sequence; every other memory is the undated one. Since **v205** it is also a real pass (`system/cross_dating.py`, ADR 0026): on every read, every still-undated moment gets one derivation attempt, strongest join first — **definitional** (the moment IS a landmark fact: a birth, a move, a graduation, or an `anchor` field that resolves to an indexed landmark exactly), then an explicit **age statement** against the birthday, then **containment** (a place or era whose span is known bounds what happened inside it). Pure, stateless, never overwrites an explicit record, never invents: a moment no anchor reaches stays honestly undated. Before v205 nothing propagated a resolved anchor at all, so a filed birthday left "Born in Redlands" undated and `keystones()` promised leverage nothing delivered. Still a term for docs and code, never a product noun.
- **Band dating** — cross-dating one level up (**v207**, ADR 0026 amendment; narrowed by **v254**, ADR 0030 decision 4). An era with no date of its own takes a span from the places lived in it (each place's own span, or the residence landmark its page names exactly), else — when the roster NAMED it after an age, "My 20s" — from the birthday, which is a definitional join and inherits the birthday's confidence. The order is deliberately not the moment ladder's: an age label is a name a roster model wrote, so it ranks under the rung grounded in what the person actually did. Derived like everything else here: never stored, never overwriting an explicit span, recomputed every read.
- **Date before you place** — the ordering rule (**v254**). The half of the cross-dating pass that needs no era runs *before* placement, so placement's own first rung — dated → age frame — can see the dates the pass derives. It ran afterwards until v254, which is why twelve of the founder's thirteen dated moments were placed by era language instead of by their dates.
- **Observed envelope** — the coverage of an era's dated members, and **never** its bounds (**v254**, ADR 0030 decision 4). Published on its own key beside the era, computed by the one definition `cross_dating.span_from_dated`. It used to be band rule 2 and it used to write the era's span, which dated eras from the accident of what got sorted into them: `high-school` read `1997/2021` off moments that were only there because the rung above it had failed. It says what the members happen to span; it does not say when the era was.
- **A floor is not a ceiling** — the rule that keeps band dating honest (**v207**). A residence union bounds an era from *inside* — it says the era at least covers that — so it is honest to display, to order the spine by and to measure a gap between eras with, and dishonest pushed back down onto the era's other moments, where one dated moment would pin every undated one to its own year. Only a span closed at both ends bounds what is inside it: an age label, or an era the person dated explicitly (`cross_dating.BAND_RULES_THAT_BOUND`).
- **Filing beat** — the moment a landmark or a placement is FILED, and the sentence the reply carries because of it (**v207**): *"Got it — that dates nine moments and your Childhood years."* The count comes from running the cross-dating pass itself over the current payload with the new record folded in, so the conversation can only claim what the next derivation will actually deliver. It is the Reading Room's own `placement_gain_sentence` clause, from one definition, brought to the two ordinary lanes that file. Never a count of what remains.
- **Derived date** — a date cross-dating worked out rather than one the person stated. Since v207 a **band** can carry one too, marked the same way. Marked by `date_derived` on the row and by nothing else (`basis == "anchor"` is not the marker — a stated anchor claim has that basis too). It carries the landmark it leaned on, the sentence the page renders where the classifier's free-text anchor used to sit ("from your birthday"), and a confidence graded by how tight the join was: a **definitional** join inherits the landmark record's own confidence, because an exact-match identity is not an estimate (owner ruling, 2026-08-24 — a certain birthday gives "11 July 1981", not "around 11 July 1981"); an **age** join keeps the hedge the person gave; **containment** is `inferred` for a place and `conjectural` for an era. The basis stays `anchor` either way, so `claim_score` keeps every stated claim above every derived one. Lives only in the derived payload, so correcting a landmark re-derives everything that leaned on it and there is no state to repair.
- **Placement** — how much of this life the timeline can actually order and place, as one number from 0 to 1 (**v208**, ADR 0027): `1 − Σwᵢ/(n·L)`, where each thing's `wᵢ` is the width in years of the interval it currently occupies and `L` is the life span. It is scored on **width, never presence** — a field-filled meter is an improper scoring rule, maximised by writing anything down, while the ladder stores a hedge as a *wider* interval, so guessing cannot pay. It is a **floor**: marginal width sums overestimate wherever ordering constraints exist, so the number says *at least this organised*, never *exactly* (`caveat_floor`). It comes as a **pair** — `score` and `score_stated`, the second computed with every derived record read as undated, so the cross-dating pass can move one and never the other. And it is shown as a **band**, one of five on fixed, arbitrary-but-stable thresholds, never as a bare percentage and never as a verdict on the life. The word is *placement*: never "completeness", never "accuracy". `timeline.placement_score`.
- **Certainty line** — the name for the whole reading the placement score belongs to: the level (*how placed is this life*) beside its margin (*what one answer is worth*), computed from **one arithmetic** — the interval-width sum `unknown_width` has ranked the plan on since v204. `next_gain` is the star re-expressed in score units, and the standing rule of ADR 0027 is that a level and its margin never come from two places, because that is the promise/delivery drift the dating-dataflow audit already found once.
- **Ghost** — what a dated moment's interval WOULD be absent its date: its era's span where the era has one, else the life (`prior_span`, stamped on the cross-dating pass's own walk for stated and derived moments alike, **v208**). It is *reconstructed, never stored* — there is no history state — and the honest consequence is that after an era's own dates improve, old ghosts **tighten** on the next read. A ghost shows today's honest reconstruction of "before", not a screenshot of what the page once said. Absent where the reconstruction is not wider than the moment's own interval: there is nothing to ghost.
- **Glow** — how much one unanswered thing would light up if it were answered, drawn from `resolves` and `leverage` on every unknown row (**v208**): the other unknowns the anchor this row would *become* would also place, and `1 + len(resolves)`, self-inclusive, so a row with no reach is exactly 1. The glow is **relative**, and its rank-quantiles are the host's job — the package supplies honest raw numbers and ranks nothing. Distinct from a **keystone**'s `gain`, which is marginal plan value against what is still unknown; row leverage is display reach, and they answer different questions.
- **Witness** — someone living who was there. Two sources, both from landmarks the person already gave: the residence ladder's own `household` rung — **no new state** — carried on every `place_no_stories` row, because the people who were in the house are exactly the people who can answer about it when the person cannot; and, since v202, the **family** domain's `living` rung (`landmarks_interaction.witness_candidates`, `timeline_data()["witnesses"]`), which is where witnesses actually come from. `living` is tri-state — only an explicit yes makes a witness, and nothing here ever invokes anybody's mortality. The Reading Room reaches the same roster from the other side: `timeline.witness_for` joins a living witness to an unknown on an edge `dependency_index` already walks — a shared source, or presence in the era whose bounds are missing. Urgency is an **ordering by generation, oldest first**, and nothing else.
- **Reading Room** — *removed 2026-09-03 (owner ruling R4a, `lifehug-platform docs/decisions/2026-09-03-timeline-unification/decision-record.md`): retired together with Go Dig and Go Deep; the interaction, `dig_plan`, research and CLI verbs are deleted in Cut 2c, and Add Landmark (an `offer` mode of `landmarks`, Cut 6a) replaces this line of work. The entry below is kept as history.* (see *Landmarks*, *Keystone*, *Whisper*) the deliberate, in-app, opt-in session where somebody sits down with what they physically have — an album, a shoebox, a folder of paperwork, a parent on speakerphone — reads it out, and the system does the arithmetic. The name is literal: an archive's reading room is where you consult materials that never leave the building, and nothing here is uploaded, scanned or ingested. The button verb is **Go Deep**. It opens with the ROOM, not the memory; it arrives with a short plan and says it once; it asks for the **precision grade** that unlocks the derivations (a school's *address* gives its district, and a district keeps records with exact years; a birthday is worth having *to the day*); and it recomputes mid-session, which is the one structural difference from every other interaction. It is the *deliberate* way the person asks themselves, where a **whisper** is the *ambient* way the loop asks and a **keystone** is the gap that makes either worth asking. Never a daily question, never a nudge, never a streak. Owner-set name, 2026-08-24. `system/research/go-deep.md`, ADR 0025.
- **Dig list** — one witness's homework: a short, plain list of what only they can answer, and what each answer would unlock. Re-derived on every compile and rendered into that person's existing `## Open Questions` wiki section — **a page, not a queue**. It carries one footer line of guidance (*"Ask about events, not processes — cue, don't correct"*) and it is never a form: "Don't send form letters… Don't ask for too much at once" is the sharpest craft warning in the whole research. There is no deferral state, no inbox, and no outstanding-item tracking; "I'll find out" is an ordinary, complete answer. A row on a witness's page is addressed to THEM, so the wiki harvester skips it and it never enters the owner's own queue (`timeline.render_dig_list`, `timeline.DIG_LIST_MARKER`).
- **Never propose a date** — the shared rule (`go-deep.md` §4.3): reporting what the arithmetic gives you is right ("you were twelve, so that puts it around 1986" states a derivation and shows its working); naming a date and asking for agreement is forbidden in every domain, including the birthday. True photographs plus suggestive interviewing produced false memories in 65–66% of participants (Lindsay et al. 2004), and a dating probe backed by the person's own evidence is that configuration exactly. ONE definition, `timeline_interaction.proposes_a_date`, run by both the timeline and landmarks lanes.
- **Specificity ladder** — the coarse-to-fine rungs inside one landmark domain, and the definition of "below target". `open` (nothing filed) · `partial` (filed, below `complete_at`) · `complete`. A `closure: user_completable` domain (family, residences, schools, work) is never complete until the person says the list is finished (`landmarks_interaction.status_for_domain`). v219: how many entries a domain holds (`collection`) is a separate declaration from what closes the list (`closure`) — one boolean used to mean both, and the domains that hold many entries without being walked lists (children, partnerships, losses, military) were on the wrong side of it.
- **Place with no stories** — a residence with a known span and no moments attached: a **story** gap, not a dating gap, and one the system could not see before a landmark named the place. `timeline.timeline_data()["place_no_stories"]`; distinct from the `place_span` unknown, which asks *when* rather than *what happened*. Since v200 it is also asked: the weekly arc planner turns it into a `place_no_stories` arc-card intent — the seventh member of `conversation.ARC_INTENT_KINDS` — which rides an ordinary conversation as a **place aside**, ranked after the timeline whisper, at most one per card, counted within the same weekly `DEFAULT_GAP_MAX`. Like any landmark question it may enter the daily queue only above the shared value threshold (owner ruling R2, 2026-09-03, `lifehug-platform docs/decisions/2026-09-03-timeline-unification/decision-record.md`; Cut 5b) and it never nags: "you lived in Costa Mesa, 1990–1993, and there are no stories from there yet; if it fits, ask what life was like there."
- **Keystone** — the single highest-leverage unknown for **this person right now**: the one date that, once known, places the most moments, computed from the dependency graph (`timeline.dependency_index` → `timeline.keystones`, at most two starred). It carries its own identity, `tl:<anchor-slug>`, and its own question, and it may become the day's question when its value clears the bar (`timeline_leverage_per_story`).
- **Landmarks** — the product word, owner-set 2026-08-23, and the same name from the surface down to the file on disk. The universal set of dating questions everyone gets (birth, **family**, residences, schooling, partnerships, children, jobs, military, losses); the skeleton that makes everything else placeable by arithmetic. Their dated results become **anchors** (the code term — see above). Shipped v199 as the sixth child interaction; `family` added in v202 — see [Timeline](#timeline) and `docs/handbook/interactions/landmarks.md`.
- **Family** — the ninth landmark domain (v202, owner-set 2026-08-24): the constellation you came from — siblings, parents, grandparents — asked second, where practitioner intake puts it (research §2.9). One entry per PERSON, ladder `who → relation → birth → living`. **Siblings' birth years are anchors**: a birth year is overlearned semantic knowledge, so it may be asked outright exactly as the person's own birthday may (`YEAR_OPENER_DOMAINS`), and it bounds *childhood*, the stretch age arithmetic can least reach. **Elders are witnesses**: a living parent or grandparent can supply the residence and school chains complete (`witness_candidates`). The *dates* are landmarks; the *people* go to the entity roster as PERSON entries carrying the relationship fact, through the existing `entity-verdict --relationship/--living` verbs — never a parallel family store.
- **Whisper** — (see [Timeline](#timeline)) information woven into a conversation that fits naturally, drawn from an arc that has developed, and serving a **second agenda beyond the conversation's primary one**. Whispers are general; the **timeline whisper** is the first kind — a keystone's real probe and the person's own landmarks, raised only where it fits, at most once per conversation, any precision accepted, never pressed. The **place aside** (v200) is the second — see *Place with no stories* above — and the two share one slot per card and one weekly budget, because a conversation carries at most one second agenda.

**How they relate:** landmarks are the universal skeleton; keystones are the
per-person gaps that skeleton leaves; whispers and keystone questions are the
two ways the loop asks.

## Eras

Two different kinds of period, kept as two different facts (ADR 0030,
2026-08-27 — see [Eras](eras.md) for the full page).

- **Age frame** — the permanent, calculated coordinate system: Childhood
  `[0,13)`, Teen years `[13,20)`, then every reached decade, derived from the
  birth origin by one definition (`cross_dating.age_frames`). Never
  model-authored, always visible, excluded from the placement score.
  **Merged**, v238/lifehug#259.
- **Named era** — a meaningful, possibly overlapping interpretation (College,
  the Mission, "Building Etherfuse") the person creates in conversation, with
  an opaque immutable **`era_id`** seeded from the creating operation, never
  from its label. `era_kind` is `stretch` (a bounded interval) or `thread`
  (a recurring presence, no natural end); flipping between them preserves
  identity and history. Dated only by what the person said — never by the
  moment envelope, roster fields, or page frontmatter. **Merged**, O-E3,
  lifehug#261, v239 (the atomic `era-record` writer and the `era` Play
  stage; membership/display are a separate, not-yet-wired seam — next).
- **Membership assertion / display decision** — the two durable receipts
  behind "this event belongs to that era" and "this is where it renders":
  independent evidence assertions feed one calculated `era_membership`, and
  a separate `era_display` decision picks where an event renders without
  ever touching chronology. **Not yet built** — `era-record` names the seam
  and refuses a payload that requests a membership rather than filing the
  rest of the act and silently dropping it; no PR is open yet.
- **Event resolution** — the deterministic binder's own receipt linking a
  date claim to the era it names, kept as a record separate from the claim
  itself so a claim can never end up resolved to two eras at once. An alias
  two eras share binds nothing and mints `identity_uncertain` rather than
  guessing. **Merged**, O-E3, lifehug#261, v239.
- **Occurrence subject scope / owner timeline relation** — who an event
  happened to, and why it belongs on the owner's own axis
  (`participated`, `lived_effect`, `contextual_only`, …), each with its own
  evidence. A stated relationship never by itself pulls a relative's
  unrelated history onto the axis. **Design decided, not yet built.**
- **Correction role** — the difference between a correction saying a source
  got something wrong (`content`, which marks the classification stale) and
  one saying only when an accepted moment happened (`placement`, which must
  not) — so dating a moment is never the reason it disappears. **In
  flight**, folded into O-C, lifehug#256.
- **`is_current`** — the one reader gate that excludes a classification
  marked stale from every derived reader immediately. **In flight**, O-C,
  lifehug#256.
- **`work_item_aliases`** — the derived map from legacy `temporal_anchor`
  work-item ids to their canonical form, so one work item keeps one
  identity across Timeline, Mirror, and the daily queue after this program.
  **In flight**, O-E6, lifehug#262.
- **`placement_reason`** — the additive field on every legacy-pass row
  answering "why is this here": which rung, what evidence, which frame or
  era, whether stale was excluded. **Design decided, not yet built.**
- **`life_view`** — the reading a node gets once placed against the life
  clip: lived history, `contradictory`, `future_plan`, or
  `subject_uncertain`. **Merged**, v238/lifehug#259.
- **`reached_frame_epoch`** — the `(reached-frame count, current band)` pair
  the publisher's signature tracks instead of the wall clock, so
  re-publishing inside one epoch is a true no-op. **Merged**,
  v238/lifehug#259.
