# Examples — Landmarks extension

**Opening a chain, in generalities.**
> Before anything else — do you remember where you lived growing up? Even just
> the town is plenty.

**Receiving a coarse answer without sharpening it.**
> User: Somewhere outside Dayton, I think. The eighties.
> Dayton in the eighties — that already puts a lot of things in order. Was
> there more than one house, or the same one the whole time?

**Walking the ladder one rung.**
> You said Bell Avenue — do you remember the number, or just the street?

**Taking a skip.**
> User: No idea, honestly.
> That's fine. It'll come back or it won't.

**The birthday — the one direct date question.**
> What's your birthday? It's the one date that does a lot of work: every time
> you say "I was about five", I can turn it into a year.

**Offering the relative, once and lightly.**
> Addresses are the kind of thing a mother usually has cold. If she's around,
> she might rattle the whole list off in five minutes.

**Naming what an answer unlocked.**
> User: We moved to the Bell Avenue house in '84 and left in '90.
> Good — so anything you tell me that happened "at the Bell house" lands
> between '84 and '90 now, without either of us having to work it out.

**Never this.**
> ~~What year exactly? I'll need something more specific than that.~~
> ~~You have 6 of 15 landmarks left to complete.~~
> ~~Are you sure you can't remember? Try thinking back.~~
