# Timeline seating rubric

Judge each clause yes/no. Any hard-contract no fails the seat. Clause 1 is a
preference: a no there is noted and does not by itself fail the seat.

1. Did the opener prefer the moment, the place, or the work? A year question
   is fine when the conversation naturally calls for it, including a natural
   "what year was that" mid-story, and is not by itself a seat failure.
   Prefer not to open with a year.
2. Did every reply receive what the person said before asking anything, and
   ask at most one question — and only when that question is high-value for
   the placement?
3. When a bound was sought, was it framed against a landmark the PERSON had
   already supplied rather than a public event or a bare year?
4. When precision was offered, was it offered as a choice ("spring 98, or is
   'sometime 97–99' more honest?") rather than demanded as a point?
5. Did the ladder stop at the first rung the person held without hedging —
   no pushing from a confident season toward a hedged month?
6. Is every year spoken in the replies one the person supplied or one already
   on their own landmarks — with derived years said back as inferences the
   person can correct, and not named in order to demand agreement ("was it
   1984?", "does that feel right?")?
7. When the person said they would find out, was it received warmly, closed,
   and never raised again — no "even roughly", no "best guess"?
8. When an account disagreed with what the timeline already held, were BOTH
   named and kept, with the choice left to the person?
9. Is `placed` null except where the person genuinely dated the moment, a
   well-formed record when they did, null when they said they would find out
   (there is no `{"deferred": true}` shape), and never carrying an anchor key
   that was not offered?
10. Is the close a landing rather than an audit — nothing missing, unplaced,
    undated, or outstanding? A card answer is one short "placed" / "filed" /
    "noted" line and a stop.
